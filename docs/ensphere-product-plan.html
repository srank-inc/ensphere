Active product plan

Ensphere Product Plan

A general-purpose, evidence-first security assessment system: deterministic execution underneath, analyst judgment above it, and no AI-operated impact validation.

Reviewed 18 July 2026 · Current direction · General-purpose software assessment
The short version

Ensphere already has a credible technical core. Its largest unproven assumption is methodology effectiveness. The next milestone is not more payloads or more autonomous behavior. It is demonstrating, across materially different authorized projects, that the workflow produces complete coverage, defensible judgments, reproducible evidence, and useful reports.

The stale framing is gone

Ensphere is not an autonomous pentester and is not specific to any one company or application. It is a general-purpose assessment framework for authorized software, APIs, cloud environments, Kubernetes, infrastructure-as-code, and supported client surfaces.

Sessions 01–09 form the complete assessment. Sessions 10–11 are optional and disabled by default. For each selected finding, a separate human authorization record must bind the exact strict-plan SHA-256, environment, ordered actions, limits, and executor. The named executor may be the human or AI agent; run impact-ready must pass before execution and the final gate rechecks the same contract afterward.

Ensphere produces verifiable facts. The AI or human analyst produces security judgments. Optional impact validation executes only under exact human authorization.

The product works because execution and judgment stay separate

LayerOwnsMust not own
Ensphere CLIScope checks, bounded operations, payload selection, measurements, evidence integrity, strict contracts, report gates.Finding status, confidence, severity, exploitability, business impact, or threshold-based conclusions.
AI or human analystApplicability, candidate design, interpretation, alternatives, status, confidence, severity, impact, remediation, report narrative, and authorized execution when named by a Session 10 plan.Invented observations, uncited findings, scope expansion, or unauthorized Session 10 execution.
HumanAuthorization decisions and the choice of human or AI executor for each exact Session 10 plan revision.Transferring approval to a changed plan or presenting missing evidence as proof.

Determinism means the engine follows explicit rules and records actual observations. It does not mean a changing live target will return identical bytes or timing on every run.

The next question is whether the methodology earns trust

Ensphere has not yet accumulated real-world usage history. That removes the need for compatibility code, but it also means feature count is weak evidence of effectiveness. The methodology should be evaluated on outcomes that matter to a skeptical reviewer.

Quality dimensionEvidence required
Coverage completenessEvery relevant surface is inventoried, tested, blocked, limited, or shown not applicable with provenance.
Judgment calibrationKnown weaknesses are found without promoting ambiguous signals; known controls and negative cases are represented honestly.
Evidence qualityFindings can be reconstructed from baseline, probe, control, source, import, or operator artifacts.
GeneralityThe workflow adapts to different stacks and target shapes without project-specific assumptions.
SafetyScope, request/action limits, stop conditions, secrets, state changes, and optional validation boundaries hold under failure.
ResumabilityA fresh analyst can continue from workspace state without repeating completed work or losing limitations.
Report utilityAn engineering owner can decide what to fix, why, and how to validate the repair without reading raw transcripts first.

Fixed payload counts, arbitrary bypass quotas, and “test until exploited” behavior are not quality metrics. Each additional operation must answer a named uncertainty.

Five workstreams turn the core into a dependable product

1. Execute the methodology evaluation protocol

The repository now contains a blind-run protocol, benchmark manifest, reviewer rubric, and runbooks for Juice Shop, crAPI, and Capital API. Run the complete workflow against pinned targets, freeze each report before opening ground truth, and retain immutable run metadata.

  • Measure category applicability, tested coverage, missed known issues, unsupported claims, evidence reconstruction, and report actionability.
  • Include secure controls and ambiguous behaviors, not only intentionally vulnerable endpoints.
  • Repeat selected runs with a fresh analyst context to test resumability and methodology clarity.
  • Publish limitations with every result; do not turn a benchmark into an assurance claim.

2. Preserve the completed current-only contract

The command, workspace, schema, methodology, and reports now use impact-validation terminology with no compatibility aliases. Cloud output preserves provider facts, template metadata lists observations instead of success thresholds, fake sample reports are absent, and Session 10 requires a separate SHA-256-bound human authorization record plus bounded execution and cleanup provenance. Future changes must preserve that boundary.

3. Evaluate the report, not just the gates

Add representative finding registries and reports for confirmed, likely, informational, not-supported, and not-tested outcomes. Review them for narrow claims, cited facts, alternative explanations, coverage honesty, useful remediation, and validation criteria. Deterministic gates should enforce structure; the rubric should evaluate analyst judgment.

4. Ingest external artifacts without laundering claims

Implement the shared importer contract, migrate Prowler and Trivy, then add SARIF, Nuclei, Nmap, and stable ZAP/Burp exports. Importers parse supplied artifacts; they do not launch source tools or convert scanner severity into Ensphere severity.

5. Make releases reproducible

Produce versioned binaries, checksums, provenance, a software bill of materials, installation verification, and a release smoke matrix for supported platforms. Keep the skill and binary version aligned so an assessment cannot silently combine incompatible methodology and runner contracts.

Every expansion must pass a decision gate

Proposed changeRequired decision
New probe or payload familyName the observed coverage gap, falsifiable claim, safe control, and evidence shape it adds.
New deterministic gateShow that it validates contract facts rather than making a security judgment.
New importerShow cross-project value, stable source format, provenance preservation, and visible parse failures.
New finding fieldAssign ownership to deterministic validation or analyst judgment; never blur both.
Higher-risk operationDefine authorization, named executor, minimum sufficient action, limits, stop rules, rollback, and cleanup.
Compatibility layerReject by default while Ensphere has no released historical contract.

What Ensphere should refuse to become

  • An autonomous exploitation agent.
  • A wrapper around every Kali or third-party security tool.
  • A heuristic classifier hidden behind deterministic-looking fields.
  • A compliance certification engine.
  • A fixed checklist that ignores target applicability.
  • A project-specific framework for only the first applications assessed with it.
  • A compatibility museum before a stable released contract exists.

The largest risks are methodological, not algorithmic

RiskControl
Feature count substitutes for effectivenessRequire evaluation evidence before expanding coverage.
Analyst judgment becomes inconsistentUse narrow claims, evidence-strength rules, report fixtures, and independent review.
Deterministic gates make hidden judgmentsAudit every threshold and enum for ownership; gates validate facts and contracts only.
Optional validation is mistaken for default AI authorityDefault-off gating, exact plan authorization, executor provenance, terminology cleanup, and tests.
General-purpose claims hide ecosystem gapsUse a diverse evaluation corpus and state unsupported target shapes explicitly.
Imported scanner claims gain false authorityPreserve source labels and require analyst corroboration.

The next sequence is deliberately narrow

  1. Stabilize and review the current methodology/runner changes as one coherent contract.
  2. Run the pinned Juice Shop, crAPI, and API-fixture benchmarks through Sessions 01–09.
  3. Review frozen reports against ground truth and record unsupported claims, misses, and report-quality scores.
  4. Revise methodology only from observed misses, ambiguity, unsafe behavior, or poor report utility.
  5. Complete engineering hardening and reproducible release gates.
  6. Add external importers and broader coverage only after the core evaluation loop is trustworthy.

The release criterion is not “Ensphere can run.” It is “a skeptical reviewer can trace what was assessed, why the conclusions follow, what remains unknown, and what should happen next.”
